02Thought becomes territory

A book becomes a unit. A unit becomes ground.

Every unit in SoTT is a real work. Its ten-part thought composition is the only thing the game reads: the strongest part chooses the role, each part sets one combat stat, and where the unit walks becomes territory.

  1. 01 · Work

    It starts with a text.

    The roster holds 40 works, most of them public-domain books catalogued by Project Gutenberg. Nothing about a unit is invented for balance; it is read from the work.

  2. 02 · Parameters

    Ten parts of thought.

    Each work carries a thought composition in the ThoughtMap parameter format: philosophy, psychology, science, morality, economy, ideology, emotion, individual, community and karma.

  3. 03 · Unit

    The strongest part chooses the role.

    The dominant parameter maps to one of ten roles, and each parameter drives exactly one stat: philosophy becomes attack, individual becomes HP, karma becomes mobility. Emotion makes a healer.

  4. 04 · Network

    Placed on a graph, not a grid.

    Five units are deployed on the nodes of a 50-node board. Allies joined by links or by shared territory resonate: stronger basic attacks and faster SP recovery, in up to three stages.

  5. 05 · Tactics

    Where it walks, it owns.

    Every node a unit moves through becomes its side’s territory and stays that way after it leaves. Holding enough of the board, for long enough, wins the battle.

StatusImplemented today: a fixed roster of 40 works whose parameters were computed ahead of time and shipped with the build. The game does not yet connect to ThoughtMap at runtime, so works added to ThoughtMap do not appear in SoTT automatically.

03The battlefield

Fifty nodes. No front line.

TACTICAL is a 5 vs 5 battle on a graph of 50 nodes and 78 links. There are no rows to hold: range, support and victory are all read from how the network is connected at that moment.

Built from the real board graph of the recorded battle (board seed 73) and its starting positions. Movement here is simplified to one link per move, with no combat; the real rules are below, and the real game is in the film.

  1. 01 · BOARD

    A graph, generated from a seed.

    Fifty octagonal nodes joined by 78 links. Normal movement follows links; every node starts neutral.

  2. 02 · TERRITORY

    Moving is claiming.

    A move claims every node on its path and can flip the enemy’s. Territory also tilts skill potency toward the side that holds more.

  3. 03 · CONNECTION

    Linked units resonate.

    Allies joined by a link or by shared territory gain up to three stages: basic attacks up to ×1.15 and SP recovery up to ×1.3.

  4. 04 · DIVISION

    Links can be cut.

    Raiders cut links and Builders create them. A unit left with no ally in its part of the network takes more damage and waits longer.

  5. 05 · INITIATIVE

    Time, not rounds.

    Every action costs Battle Time according to the action and the unit’s speed. Whoever is ready next acts next.

  6. 06 · VICTORY

    Three ways to end it.

    Eliminate the other side; or hold 23 of 50 nodes with a 4-node lead for 3 BT; or be ahead when the battle reaches 180 BT or 1,000 commands.

Every command shows its consequence first.

Choose a unit, choose a command, read the forecast, then execute. These clips are the real interface at the game’s own timing.

    Take ground on the way.

    Reachable nodes light up along the links. Inside your own territory a unit travels freely, so ground taken early becomes speed later.

    Spend SP where it counts.

    A skill’s target window shows range, cost, cooldown and the computed result before you commit. Here a Sniper’s LONGSHOT reaches across the board.

  3. 03 · Impact

    Feel which blow mattered.

    Attackers lunge, the clock holds for an instant, the target recoils. Three weights of impact: normal, strong, and the blow that defeats. There are no random criticals.

  4. 04 · Buff / Debuff

    Status with a clock on it.

    Effects are measured in Battle Time and show when they will end. An Interferer’s DISRUPT pushes an enemy’s next turn back; the panel shows by how much.

  5. 05 · Healer

    Mend beside you, heal at range.

    In the current build a Supporter’s basic action is MEND: free, but only for a wounded ally on an adjacent cell. Its Ranged Heal reaches two links away for SP, and overflow becomes a barrier.

    Hold the board, win the battle.

    The HUD tracks both sides’ nodes against the 23-node mark. Hold the lead for 3 Battle Time and the battle ends on territory, whoever is still standing.

06AI plays tactics

A local language model, playing by the rules.

As an experiment, an AI VTuber system was connected to SoTT as an external player. A local Qwen3 4B model builds a squad, places it, and then commands it for a whole battle while the characters commentate.

  • 76legal actions chosen by the model in the accepted battle, start to finish
  • 10formation and placement decisions made before the battle
  • 150commands in that battle, re-simulated from the replay with an identical result
  • 0invalid commands, substituted moves or automatic time-outs on the model’s side

  • Human

    Starts and stops.

    A person launches the session and can halt it. During the recorded battle no game command was entered by hand.

  • Enemy AI

    The game’s own opponent.

    Team B is played by the AI built into SoTT. It scores every legal command and searches routes toward territory. It is not the language model.

  • Game agent

    The external player.

    Team A is played by Qwen3 4B running locally. It reads the observed state and picks one command from the legal list the game offers.

  1. Observed state
  2. Legal commands
  3. Model chooses
  4. Engine validates
  5. Replay log

What this does not show

  • Legal is not the same as good. The model lost this battle on territory, and all five battles of a longer series.
  • The commentary sometimes speculates beyond what the game state shows.
  • Long unattended sessions have not been verified.
